Our project focuses on predicting HVAC runtime to better understand key drivers of energy consumption. The dataset consists of two distinct sources: 100 event-driven indoor logs capturing system behavior and regularly sampled outdoor environmental data. Integrating these sources required careful alignment due to differences in timing and structure. We evaluated multiple modeling approaches, including rolling window techniques to capture temporal dependencies, as well as tree-based methods such as XGBoost and LightGBM. These approaches were chosen to balance interpretability and predictive performance while accounting for nonlinear relationships in the data. Our results show that tree based models are the most effective, with LightGBM's R^2 of .7705 outperforming a naive prediction's R^2 of .696. In contrast, the rolling window performed close to the naive prediction, with an R^2 of .706. While more work is needed, these results suggest that machine learning could be used in order to enhance HVAC control strategies for customers.
